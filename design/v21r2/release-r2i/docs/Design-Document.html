Treat these as an order-of-magnitude check, not a performance guarantee. Real throughput will be lower: shunt current through the seawater beside the plates, end losses at the inlet and outlet, and bubble void fraction from electrolysis all reduce the ideal numbers. The v21 boat test measured 0.2–0.3 m/s with a straight outlet — that is the number to design the hull around.

5.1 Why the flux path matters more than the magnet grade

Every millimetre of non-magnetic material between magnet and steel is in series with the whole circuit. The cap is 1.0 mm and the steel plate is directly above it, so the gap is 1.0 mm of plastic. Doubling that cap to 2 mm would drop the bore field by roughly a fifth. This is also why nothing compressible goes on the cap face — foam there is an air gap wearing a plastic hat.

7. How the yoke retains the tray

Vertically the assembly is a stack: tray rim → steel plate (z 19.5), mirrored tray butted at z 0. Bolting the halves closes that stack, so the plastic cannot move up or down once the lugs are tight. Lengthwise, the bellmouth and rear block pin the tray at both ends through the pegs and electrode pockets. Sideways there is 0.15 mm a side of clearance and no clamp — the bolts never squeeze the plastic, they only pull lug to lug.

If the tray rattles sideways, a 0.3–0.5 mm closed-cell foam strip on the outer wall faces will take it up. Do not put foam between cap and steel plate: that surface is the magnet's path into the yoke. Do not use open-cell foam anywhere in the assembly — it wicks brine into the cavity.
