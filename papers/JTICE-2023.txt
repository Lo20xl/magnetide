Background: The magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) are highly relevant to practical applications, including marine 
propulsion and hydrogen production etc. For better implementation to applications, typical characteristics of 
the MHD bubbly jet, such as the size distribution and motion of oxygen and hydrogen bubbles, need to be well 
realized.
Methods: The MHD bubbly jet generated by high current density associated with strong permanent magnet is 
experimentally investigated. Two ﬁelds of view are taken to analyze the details of micro-sized bubbles and the 
whole pattern of bubbly jet. To couple the magnetoelectric eﬀects and bubble dynamics, the Reynolds number 
based on the Lorentz force 𝑅𝑒𝐿is applied in the parametric analysis.
Findings: Dependence of the mean size of bubble on the current density is generally veriﬁed. Two regimes 
regarding the bubble sizes are distinguished. The proposed mechanisms of these two regimes are supported 
by the pattern of bubbly jet. For 𝑅𝑒𝐿≤2000, size of bubble monotonically increases with current density, so 
that referred to as the electrochemistry regime. Nevertheless, the size of bubble remains nearly unchanged for 
𝑅𝑒𝐿> 2000, referred to as the ﬂow regime in which the turbulent breakup of the bubbly jet core starts to evolve.
1. Introduction
Magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) is a unique branch of ﬂuid dynam-
ics dealing with conducting ﬂuids driven by magnetoelectric eﬀects of 
Lorentz force. Applications of MHD had been interests of engineers 
since its initiation in 1940s [1], including marine propulsion [2,3], 
pumps [4,5], motors [6], and silicon growth [7,8]. In the meantime, 
in the ﬁelds of enhanced heat transfer [9–13] and atmospheric pres-
sure plasma jet [14,15], MHD is often applied in which the conductive 
medium is nanoﬂuid and plasma, respectively.
More recently, because of the concern of greenhouse eﬀect, produc-
tion and utility of hydrogen energy is a truly important subject [16]. 
Hydrogen is typically produced by alkaline water electrolysis [17,18]
because pure hydrogen can be generated by relatively simple equip-
ment. Nevertheless, the yield rate of oxygen and hydrogen bubbles is 
low due to adhesion of the bubbles on the electrodes to reduce the re-
action rate [19,20]. To improve the production rate of hydrogen, the 
Lorentz force of MHD has been successfully applied to enhance mass 
transfer and yield better eﬃciency [21–23]. Comprehensive reviews

regarding the application of magnetic ﬁeld to enhance hydrogen pro-
duction can be referred to Ref. [24,25].
The characteristics of bubbles in electrolysis are also important 
issues studied intensively [26–32]. During electrolysis, the so-called 
bubble eﬀect, i.e., adhesion and accumulation of bubbles near the elec-
trodes, reduces the reaction rate. As a result, the covering area and 
dispersion speed of bubbles are important to increase hydrogen produc-
tion [24]. In pure electrolysis, growth rate and the detached speed of 
big bubbles are vital in determining bubble size [27]. The bubble de-
tachment diameter is inversely proportional to the ﬂow velocity, but 
proportional to the current density [29]. After 10 seconds of electroly-
sis, diameter of oxygen bubble is about 55 μm to 170 μm for current 
density 0.5 A/cm2 to 4 A/cm2, respectively. Assisted by MHD con-
vection, the bubbles rise faster to improve surface coverage of the 
electrode [26]. In addition to acceleration of bubble, the size of bub-
ble increases with higher current density (or the Lorentz force) [28]. 
Removal of bubbles from the electrode plates decreases signiﬁcantly by 
the presence of MHD eﬀect [30]. The bubble size for current density 
of 2.3 A∕cm2 ∼3 A∕cm2 after 0.4 second is around 250 μm ∼300 μm.

On the other hand, conventional jet ﬂow had been thoroughly inves-
tigated for decades as reviewed in Ref. [33]. The formation of turbulent 
jet is generally described as three main regions, i.e., the potential core, 
the developing region and the self-preservation region [34,35]. Also in-
tensively studied is the two phase jet, e.g., liquid jet associated with 
atomizing gas stream [36,37]. A ﬂapping instability is observed in an 
incompletely atomized liquid jet, in which regime of the ﬂapping insta-
bility is determined by the wavelength and liquid jet diameter [36]. The 
MHD saltwater jet diﬀers from the conventional liquid-gas jet by two 
features, i.e., driven by the Lorentz force and chemical reactions asso-
ciated with electrolysis. While the Lorentz force can be used for marine 
vehicle propulsion, electrolysis may be potentially applied for hydrogen 
collection. To study the coupling eﬀect of bubble generation by elec-
trolysis with hydrodynamics, forced saltwater ﬂow with electrolysis is 
experimented [38]. Bubble size is found to be controlled by the cur-
rent density. Larger bubbles are observed for stronger current density 
or slower ﬂow. Recent study of MHD bubbly jet found the ﬂow tran-
sition from laminar to turbulent is determined by a modiﬁed Reynolds

Most of the reported MHD works of electrolyzed bubbles mainly 
focus on the application of enhanced hydrogen production, which the 
Lorentz force orients in line with buoyancy. On the other hand, size and 
motion of bubbles consisted in a bubbly MHD jet, in which the Lorentz 
force orients perpendicularly to buoyancy, are rarely studied. In addi-
tion, the MHD jet not just provides thrust needed for marine propulsion, 
but also produces bubbles of hydrogen and oxygen by associated elec-
trolysis. These produced bubbles might possess further implications to 
practical applications. For instance, the hydrogen may be collected for 
possible source as supplemental energy in a hybrid power system. Alter-
natively, bubbles may be collected and guided to surround the sailing 
marine vehicle for drag reduction. Better understandings of the pro-
duced bubbles in a MHD bubbly jet are important to optimize these 
applications. In this paper, the coupling eﬀect regarding the size and 
motion of electrolyzed bubbles in the Lorentz-force-driven MHD bubbly 
jet is presented by parametric analysis of experimental results.

Since the main interest is to understand the characteristics and mo-
tion of bubbles, bubbles are treated as the same gas component with 
a constant surface tension (𝛾) to the saltwater. To analyze the detailed 
characteristics of bubble and formation of the whole bubbly jet, two top 
views are recorded by CCD cameras, referred to as ﬁeld of view 1 (FoV-
1) and ﬁeld of view 2 (FoV-2) shown in Fig. 1. Images of FoV-1, taken 
by a Mikrotron MotionBlitz camera with a frame speed of 100 fps, fo-
cus on the close-up view of bubbles. On the other hand, a GoPro Action 
(60 fps) is used to observe the full view of bubbly jet covered by the 
FoV-2. The recorded pictures are transmitted to computers PC-1 and 
PC-2, respectively, and imported into the Matlab software for further 
analysis. The main parameters analyzed include the mean diameter and 
the Sauter diameter of bubbles, denoted as 𝐷𝑚and 𝐷𝑆, respectively. To 
gain insights of bubble motion, diameter and speed of bubbles, denoted 
respectively as 𝐷𝑏and 𝑈𝑏, will also be measured and discussed.
3. Results and discussion
3.1. Distributions of bubble size
This section focuses on the characteristics of bubbles produced by 
electrolysis and transported by the Lorentz force. Shown in Fig. 2 are 
the representative images taken in FoV-1 for various current strengths 
𝐼= 2 A, 4 A, 6 A and 7 A with a channel geometry of 𝑤 = 4 mm and 
ℎ=5 mm. Bubbles of various sizes are clearly observed. Based on visual 
inspection, the overall size of bubbles tends to increase as the current 
strength is raised higher. Similar trend is also observed for the cases 
in a deeper height of ℎ = 10 mm as shown in Fig. 3. Nevertheless, the 
bubbles for 𝐼= 2 A and 4 A in this deeper channel appear smaller than 
the correspondent cases of identical current strength shown in Fig. 2. 
It is noticed that, the sizes of bubbles are visibly comparable for cases 
of 𝐼= 2 A shown in Fig. 2 and 𝐼= 4 A shown in Fig. 3, whose current 
densities 𝐽are nearly identical. This indicates that bubble size is more 
relevant to 𝐽which directly governs the Lorentz force, rather than 𝐼. 
Quantitative veriﬁcations of these qualitative observations will be pre-
sented in latter sections.
The ﬁrst quantitative measure interested is the numbers of bubbles. 
Shown in Fig. 4 is the average bubble count 𝑛within the FoV-1 taken be-
tween 5 s < 𝑡 < 30 s (2500 frames in total) for various current strengths 
and current densities. In general, 𝑛does not vary signiﬁcantly, e.g., 
𝑛 ≈200 as shown in Fig. 4, except for the cases of very low 𝐼(or 𝐽) 
when the Lorentz-induced ﬂow is weak. Similar trends regarding the 
size and number of bubbles are observed for a wider 𝑤 = 6 mm associ-
ated with various ℎand 𝐼, e.g., ℎ = 5 mm and 10 mm, 𝐼= 2, 4 A, 6 A 
and 7 A. The representative snapshots are shown in Fig. 5.

Size of bubbles is one of the main concerns when investigating the 
bubble dynamics. The mean diameter of bubbles 𝐷𝑚is measured be-
tween 25 s < 𝑡 < 30 s (500 frames) from three diﬀerent experiments 
under identical conditions. Shown in Fig. 6 are the mean diameters 
in various current strength and current density. Sizes of bubbles un-
der the present experimental conditions are in 𝑂(100) μm. Since the 
width 𝑤does not aﬀect the overall current density, so that its eﬀect 
to 𝐷𝑚is insigniﬁcant. 𝐷𝑚increases as 𝐼is raised higher as shown 
in Fig. 6(a). Nevertheless, the increase of bubble size seems less dra-
matic for higher current strength. Also observed in the range of weaker 
current strength 𝐼≤4 A, the bubbles appear apparently smaller for a 
deeper channel of ℎ = 10 mm. On the other hand, the mean diame-
ter 𝐷𝑚can be nicely described for various experimental conditions if 
plotted versus current density 𝐽as shown in Fig. 6(b). Two distinct 
regimes can be clearly distinguished, such that: an apparent increase 
of bubble size for 𝐽< 104 A∕m2 until asymptotically reaching to nearly 
constant size for 𝐽> 104 A∕m2. The asymptotic diameter 𝐷𝑚≈270 μm
is in line with the early ﬁndings even the conﬁguration of MHD eﬀect 
is not identical [30]. Similar behavior is also observed for the Sauter 
diameter 𝐷𝑆as shown in Fig. 7. It is noticed that, even the trend of 
larger bubbles generated by stronger current density has been well re-
ported previously [29,30,38], those early experiments do not vary the 
area of electrodes to verify the general dependence shown in Fig. 6(b). 
In addition, existence of the distinct regimes suggests diﬀerent domi-
nant mechanisms within these two regimes, hereinafter referred to as 
(1) the electrochemistry regime and (2) the ﬂow regime, respectively, 
as the reasons addressed in the latter sections.
It has been reported that, size of bubble appears larger for either 
stronger current density or slower forced ﬂow speed [38]. In the present 
study, ﬂow is induced by the Lorentz force, so that the speed is also 
proportional to the current density. This conﬁrms the bubble size can 
be well described by the current density as shown in Figs. 6(b) and 
Fig. 7(b). Nevertheless, two distinct regimes, i.e., the electrochemistry 
regime and the ﬂow regime, are identiﬁed. Within the electrochem-
istry regime of 𝐽< 104 A∕m2, bubble size increases as current density is 
raised higher. On the other hand, the bubble size maintains nearly un-
changed in the ﬂow regime, once the current density exceeds 104 A∕m2. 
The reason of observed regime transition of bubble size is discussed in 
the next section.

the ﬂow is not the dominant factor. On the contrast, once 𝑅𝑒𝐿> 2000, 
the bubble sizes appear nearly the same for all conditions regardless the 
depth of channel ℎ. In this regime, the ﬂow parameter of 𝑅𝑒𝐿is capa-
ble to solely describe the bubble size, so that it is referred to as the ﬂow 
regime.

4. Conclusion
Magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) jet driven by the Lorentz force is 
relevant to the potential applications, such as marine propulsion and 
hydrogen production. The present experimental study analyzes the 
characteristics of MHD bubbly jet, such as the size distributions of bub-
bles produced in electrolysis. Under a ﬁxed magnetic ﬁeld at about 
𝐵0 ≈0.147 T associated with current density 𝐽≤2.5 × 104 A∕m2, the 
size of gaseous bubbles of oxygen and hydrogen is determined by the 
current density and ranges from 𝑂(10) μm ∼𝑂(100) μm. For condition 
associated with relatively weaker current density, mean bubble size is 
larger as the current density is increased. However, once the current 
density reaches suﬃciently high, e.g., 𝐽≈104 A∕m2, the size remains

To elucidate the coupling eﬀect of the Lorentz force and the electrol-
ysis to the size of bubble, mean diameter of bubbles versus a modiﬁed 
Reynolds number 𝑅𝑒𝐿is analyzed. Similar behavior is observed, such 
that larger bubbles are produced for higher 𝑅𝑒𝐿until maximum bubble 
size is reached for 𝑅𝑒𝐿> 2, 000. Nevertheless, even bubble size grows 
for increasing 𝑅𝑒𝐿for 𝑅𝑒𝐿≤2, 000, the size appears diﬀerent for various 
current density. The size diﬀerence indicates the mean bubble diameter 
can not be solely described by the ﬂow pattern in this region of 𝑅𝑒𝐿. 
Dynamics of bubble motion are also investigated, which is presented 
by the bubble-based Reynolds number 𝑅𝑒𝑏. In addition to higher bub-
ble speed (or 𝑅𝑒𝑏) for increasing 𝑅𝑒𝐿as expected, merges and bursts of 
bubbles are often observed under suﬃciently high 𝑅𝑒𝐿.
Inability of 𝑅𝑒𝐿to universally correlate the bubble size suggests 
the distribution of bubble size is not fully aﬀected by hydrodynamics 
in the present MHD ﬂow. Two regimes, referred to as the electro-
chemistry regime and the ﬂow regime, are categorized. For condition 
of relative weaker magnetoelectric eﬀect, i.e., smaller current density 
or 𝑅𝑒𝐿≤2000, size of bubble increases with the magnitude of cur-
rent density. The electrochemistry regime is referred for this signiﬁcant 
dependence between bubble size and current density. Nevertheless, 
for suﬃciently strong magnetoelectric eﬀect (high current density or 
𝑅𝑒𝐿> 2000), breakup of jet core is triggered by turbulence. The mean 
size of bubbles remains nearly unchanged regardless the magnitude of 
applied electric ﬁeld. Since the constant bubble size is mainly aﬀected 
by the ﬂow transition of turbulent breakups, so that referred to as the 
ﬂow regime. The ﬂow transition is supported by the corresponding pat-
tern of bubbly jet.
